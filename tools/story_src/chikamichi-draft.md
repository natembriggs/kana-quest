# 近道 / The Shortcut — draft (L6, G6, single chapter)

Written by Claude Opus 5.5. Original story; no source text.

Genre: grounded mountain survival. Protagonist: 大輝 (Daiki), 17, in his high
school's mountaineering club. With him: his brother 翔 (Shō), 11.

## Scene map

1. **Trailhead, Saturday morning in October.** Daiki and Shō get off the first
   bus. Setup: Mum only let them go after Daiki promised to phone "if anything
   happens"; Shō has no boots, only new white trainers; Daiki skips the hiking
   plan box ("it's a day trip"); Shō asks what to do if lost and Daiki, showing
   off, recites his club's rule: *if you're lost, don't go down — climb.*
   Streams end at waterfalls and cliffs; ridges have paths and phone signal.
   Consequence: the rule and the two cut corners are on the table.
2. **Summit, after one; descent from half past two.** They are an hour behind
   because Shō is slow and Daiki kept filming. The last bus leaves at 4:40 —
   tight. Missing it means phoning Mum for a two-hour rescue drive and never
   being trusted with Shō again. Goal: catch the bus without asking for help.
   Change: at a bend, a faint path with a faded pink tape heads straight down
   toward a forest road on the map. Daiki takes the shortcut over Shō's doubt.
3. **In the woods, around half past three.** The path vanishes under leaves;
   the map app shows a blue dot on grey, no signal, 12 % battery (the videos).
   Daiki pushes on; Shō slips in his trainers and hurts his ankle. The sun is
   behind the ridge; it is getting dark and cold. They hear water. Shō wants to
   follow the stream down. Daiki remembers his own rule, but the water is close
   and the ridge is far. "Just to look."
4. **The waterfall, about four.** They follow the stream down over wet rocks
   until the ground ends: a drop of nearly ten metres, a cliff of wet roots
   beside it, and far below, the pale line of the forest road. Shō begs to
   climb down. The temptation is double: it might work, and if they get down
   alone nobody ever has to know about the shortcut or the missing form.
   Going up means signal — and confessing everything. Daiki weighs the wet
   roots against Shō's ankle in the dark, and asks Shō what he said that
   morning. The choice: climb.
5. **The climb back, dusk into night.** Shō cries; Daiki carries both packs,
   saves the phone light for the call, climbs by moonlight tree by tree ("ten
   more trees"). After over an hour: level ground, cold wind, a red paint mark
   — the ridge and the real trail. One bar, 4 %. He starts to call Mum, stops,
   calls the police instead and tells them everything, including his own
   mistakes. Told to stay put; rescuers are coming; Mum will be told. 2 % left.
6. **The ridge, late night.** They huddle under one windbreaker. Shō: "You'll
   get told off." Daiki: "Only me." Shō: "But you did climb." After eleven,
   lights come along the ridge calling their names; Daiki holds up the light he
   saved and calls "Here! Please help us!" — the words he'd spent all day
   avoiding, and they come easily.

The ending turns on the choice at the waterfall, which the opening sets up
three ways: the rule, the promise to phone, and the skipped hiking plan.

## Japanese (prose)

十月の土曜日、朝一番のバスが山の登山口に着いた。降りたのは、大輝と弟の翔の二人だけだった。大輝は十七歳で、高校の山岳部に入っている。翔は十一歳で、山に登るのは今日が初めてだった。登山靴を持っていないので、買ったばかりの白いスニーカーをはいてきた。母は最初、二人だけで山に行くことに反対したが、大輝が「何かあったら、すぐ電話する」と約束して、やっと許してくれた。

登山口の看板の横には、登山届を入れる白い箱があった。「これ、書かないの？」と翔が聞いた。「日帰りだから、いいよ」と言って、大輝は箱の前を通り過ぎた。歩き出してすぐ、翔が「ねえ、山で道に迷ったら、どうするの？」と聞いた。大輝は、部の先輩たちがいつも言っている言葉を、少し得意になって教えた。「迷ったら、下りるな。登れ」。「山の中の川に沿って下りると、たいてい滝や崖に出るけど、尾根には道があるし、電話もつながるんだ」。翔は「ふうん」と言っただけで、すぐにどんぐりを拾い始めた。

頂上に着いたのは、予定より一時間も遅い、一時半だった。翔の足が遅かったからでもあり、大輝が何度も立ち止まって、スマホで動画を撮っていたからでもあった。最終バスは、四時四十分に登山口を出る。二時半に頂上を出たので、地図に書いてある時間どおりに歩いても、ぎりぎりだった。バスに乗れなかったら、母に電話して、二時間かけて車で迎えに来てもらうしかない。「だから言ったでしょう」という母の声が、もう聞こえるようだった。そうなったら、もう二度と翔を連れて山に来られないだろう。

三時少し前、道が大きく曲がる所で、大輝は林の中をまっすぐ下りていく細い道に気がついた。入り口の木の枝には、色のあせたピンクのテープが結んであった。地図を見ると、ずっと下のほうに林道が通っている。「近道だ。ここを下りれば、三十分は早い」。「でも、地図に道、ないよ」と翔が言った。「テープがあるだろ。誰かが使ってる道なんだよ」。大輝はそう言って、先に林の中へ入っていった。

最初ははっきりしていた道も、落ち葉が深くなるにつれて、だんだん見えなくなった。二十分ほどたつと、テープも道も、どこにも見当たらなかった。スマホの地図アプリを開くと、青い点は出たが、そのまわりは灰色のままだった。画面の上には「圏外」と出ていて、バッテリーは十二パーセントしか残っていなかった。動画を撮りすぎたのだ。「兄ちゃん、戻ろうよ」と翔が言った。「もう少しだけ。林道はすぐ下のはずだから」。斜面がだんだん急になってきたとき、後ろで短い声がした。振り返ると、翔が落ち葉の上に座り込んで、右の足首を押さえていた。スニーカーの底が、ぬれた葉の上ですべったのだ。歩けないほどではなかったが、翔は一歩ごとに顔をしかめた。気がつくと、太陽はもう尾根の向こうに隠れていて、林の中は急に暗く、寒くなっていた。

そのとき、下のほうから水の音が聞こえてきた。「川だ。川に沿って下りたら、町に出られるよね」と翔が言った。大輝の頭に、朝、自分が言った言葉が浮かんだ。でも、水の音はすぐそこで、尾根ははるか上だった。「……見るだけだ」と大輝は言った。

川は細く、大きな石の間を白い水が流れていた。二人は、ぬれた石に手をつきながら、川に沿って下っていった。十分ほど進むと、水の音が急に大きくなった。数歩先で、川がふっと消えていた。水は岩のふちから、十メートル近く下へ落ちていた。滝の横は崖で、ぬれた木の根が何本か、岩の間から出ているだけだった。滝のずっと下のほうに、白っぽい林道の線が見えた。「あそこだ！兄ちゃん、あの根っこにつかまって下りれば、すぐだよ！」。

翔の言うとおりかもしれない、と大輝は思った。部活で岩場の練習はしたことがあるから、自分一人なら、十メートルくらいは下りられるかもしれない。それに、ここを自分たちだけで下りてしまえば、近道をしたことも、登山届を出さなかったことも、誰にも知られずにすむ。尾根まで戻れば電話はつながるだろうが、そのときは母にも警察にも、全部話さなければならない。大輝は、滝のふちと翔の足首を、交互に見た。暗くなりかけた崖で、片手でぬれた根をつかみ、もう片方の手で弟を支えている自分を想像した。手が一度すべったら、それで終わりだ。「翔。朝、俺、何て言った？」。翔はしばらく黙っていた。「……迷ったら、下りるな。登れ」。「そうだ。登るぞ」。

翔は泣き出した。「なんで？林道、すぐそこなのに！」。大輝は答えずに、翔のリュックを自分の胸の前にかけ、弟の口にチョコレートを一つ入れた。登りは、下りの何倍も長かった。大輝は木の幹を一本ずつつかんで体を引き上げ、それから振り返って翔の手を引いた。バッテリーを電話のために残しておきたくて、スマホのライトはつけなかった。幸い月が出ていて、木の間から少しだけ光が入ってきた。翔は何度も「もう無理」と言い、そのたびに大輝は「あと十本」と言って、木を数えさせた。

一時間以上登ったころ、急に足元が平らになった。冷たい風が顔に当たった。尾根だった。岩には、登山道を示す赤いペンキの丸が塗ってあった。スマホを出すと、アンテナが一本立っていて、バッテリーは四パーセントだった。大輝は母の番号を出しかけてやめ、警察に電話をかけた。声が震えないように、大輝はひとつずつ話した。弟と二人でいること、弟が足をけがしていること、自分で決めて地図にない道に入ったこと、登山届を出していないこと。警察の人は最後まで聞いてから、「そこから動かないでください。今から救助隊がそちらへ向かいます」と言った。「お母さんにも、こちらから連絡します」。電話を切ると、バッテリーは二パーセントになっていた。

二人は大きな岩の横に座り、一枚のウインドブレーカーに一緒にくるまった。「兄ちゃん、怒られるね」と翔が小さな声で言った。「怒られるのは、俺だけだ」。翔は少し考えてから、「でも、兄ちゃん、ちゃんと登ったじゃん」と言った。夜の十一時を過ぎたころ、尾根の向こうから、ヘッドライトの光がいくつも揺れながら近づいてきた。「大輝くーん！翔くーん！」と呼ぶ声が聞こえた。大輝は立ち上がり、残しておいたバッテリーでライトをつけて、スマホを高く上げた。「ここです！助けてください！」。一日中言えなかったその言葉は、口に出してみると、思っていたよりずっと簡単だった。

## English

On a Saturday in October, the first bus of the morning pulled in at the trailhead. Only two people got off: Daiki and his younger brother Shō. Daiki was seventeen and in his high school’s mountaineering club. Shō was eleven, and today was the first time he had ever climbed a mountain. He had no hiking boots, so he had come in brand-new white trainers. At first their mother had been against the two of them going to the mountains alone, but when Daiki promised, “If anything happens, I’ll call right away,” she finally said yes.

Beside the sign at the trailhead was a white box for hiking plans. “Aren’t you going to fill one in?” Shō asked. “It’s a day trip, it’s fine,” said Daiki, and walked past the box. They had barely set off when Shō asked, “Hey, what do you do if you get lost in the mountains?” A little proudly, Daiki passed on what the older members of his club always said. “If you’re lost, don’t go down. Climb.” “If you follow a stream down through the mountains, you usually end up at a waterfall or a cliff. But the ridges have paths, and your phone works up there.” Shō just said “Huh,” and soon started picking up acorns.

They reached the summit at half past one, a full hour later than planned. It was partly because Shō was slow, and partly because Daiki kept stopping to film videos on his phone. The last bus left the trailhead at 4:40. They left the summit at half past two, so even walking at the times on the map, it would be tight. If they missed the bus, the only option was to phone their mother and have her spend two hours driving out to fetch them. He could already hear her saying, “I told you so.” If that happened, he would never be allowed to bring Shō to the mountains again.

Just before three, at a sharp bend in the trail, Daiki noticed a narrow path running straight down through the woods. A faded pink tape was tied to a branch where it began. According to the map, a forest road ran far below. “A shortcut. If we go down here, we’ll save half an hour at least.” “But the path isn’t on the map,” Shō said. “There’s tape, isn’t there? Someone uses this path.” With that, Daiki led the way into the woods.

The path had been clear at first, but as the fallen leaves grew deeper, it slowly disappeared. After about twenty minutes, there was no sign of either the tape or the path. When he opened the map app on his phone, a blue dot appeared, but everything around it stayed grey. At the top of the screen it said “No service”, and there was only twelve percent battery left. He had filmed too many videos. “Let’s go back,” Shō said. “Just a bit further. The forest road must be right below us.” As the slope grew steeper, there was a short cry behind him. He turned round to find Shō sitting in the leaves, clutching his right ankle. The sole of his trainer had slipped on the wet leaves. He could still walk, but he winced at every step. Daiki suddenly realised that the sun had already gone behind the ridge, and the woods had turned dark and cold.

Just then, they heard water somewhere below. “A river! If we follow it down, we’ll come out at a town, won’t we?” said Shō. The words Daiki had said that morning came back to him. But the water was right there, and the ridge was far above. “…Just to look,” Daiki said.

The stream was narrow, white water running between big rocks. The two of them made their way down along it, steadying themselves with their hands on the wet stones. After about ten minutes, the sound of the water suddenly grew louder. A few steps ahead, the stream simply vanished. The water fell nearly ten metres over the edge of the rock. Beside the waterfall was a cliff, with nothing but a few wet tree roots poking out between the rocks. Far below the falls, they could see the pale line of the forest road. “There it is! If we hang on to those roots and climb down, we’re nearly there!”

Maybe Shō was right, Daiki thought. He had practised on rock with the club, so on his own he might manage ten metres or so. Besides, if they got down by themselves, nobody would ever find out about the shortcut, or about the hiking plan he hadn’t handed in. On the ridge the phone would probably work, but then he would have to tell everything, to his mother and to the police. Daiki looked from the edge of the falls to Shō’s ankle and back again. He pictured himself on the darkening cliff, gripping a wet root with one hand and holding his brother up with the other. One slip of a hand and that would be it. “Shō. What did I say this morning?” Shō said nothing for a while. “…If you’re lost, don’t go down. Climb.” “Right. We’re climbing.”

Shō burst into tears. “Why? The road’s right there!” Daiki didn’t answer. He hung Shō’s backpack on his own chest, and put a piece of chocolate in his brother’s mouth. Going up took many times longer than coming down. Daiki grabbed the trees one trunk at a time to pull himself up, then turned round and pulled Shō up by the hand. He wanted to save the battery for a call, so he didn’t turn on his phone’s light. Luckily the moon was out, and a little light came in between the trees. Shō kept saying “I can’t,” and every time, Daiki said “Ten more trees,” and made him count them.

After more than an hour of climbing, the ground under their feet suddenly levelled out. A cold wind hit his face. It was the ridge. On a rock was a red painted circle, the mark of the hiking trail. When he took out his phone, it showed one bar of signal, and the battery was at four percent. Daiki started to bring up his mother’s number, stopped, and called the police instead. Trying to keep his voice steady, Daiki told them one thing at a time. That he was with his brother, that his brother had hurt his foot, that he had chosen to take a path that wasn’t on the map, and that he hadn’t handed in a hiking plan. The officer heard him out, then said, “Don’t move from where you are. A rescue team is on its way to you now.” “We’ll contact your mother as well.” When he hung up, the battery was down to two percent.

The two of them sat down beside a big rock and wrapped themselves together in one windbreaker. “You’re going to get told off,” Shō said in a small voice. “I’m the only one who’s getting told off.” Shō thought for a moment, then said, “But you did climb, though.” Some time after eleven at night, a cluster of headlamps came bobbing towards them along the ridge. They heard voices calling, “Daiki! Shō!” Daiki stood up, used the battery he had saved to switch on the light, and held his phone up high. “Here! Please help us!” The words he hadn’t been able to say all day turned out, once he said them aloud, to be far easier than he had thought.
