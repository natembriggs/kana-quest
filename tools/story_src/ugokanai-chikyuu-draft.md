# 動かない地球 / The Earth That Stays Put — draft (L3, G3)

Original single-chapter science-fiction adventure. Written by Claude Opus 5.5.

## Science used (all set up before the payoff)

- From the Moon's near side the Earth stays (almost) fixed in the sky — it
  never rises or sets. (Libration wobbles it only a few degrees.) The Sun, by
  contrast, does cross the Moon's sky, slowly.
- No wind or rain on the Moon, so tyre tracks never disappear — which means
  the ground around a busy base is covered in them.
- A lunar night lasts about two weeks, so solar charging stops at sunset.
- Radio on the Moon needs line of sight: a hill blocks it.
- Over a trip of a few kilometres the direction to the Earth does not change.

## Scene map

1. **Chie's room, the base, a year after arriving.** Chie (12) lives on a Moon
   base with her mother. From her window: a grey hill, and above it the blue
   Earth, which never moves. She has watched it every day for a year. Below
   the window, the ground is full of rover tracks that never fade, because
   there is no wind or rain. *(Sets up both facts as her own observation.)*
2. **Departure, the last day before the two-week night.** Mom must check the
   solar panels over the hill *behind* the base before night; Chie goes along
   in the rover. Mom warns that past the hill the radio can't reach the base.
   As they leave, Chie looks back: the Earth is over the base.
3. **The panel field.** They cross the hill and drive straight; Mom fixes a
   broken panel. On the way back the rover's screen dies: no map. The battery
   has about an hour, and with night coming it can't recharge.
   *(Goal: get home. Obstacle: no map, no radio, limited power.)*
4. **Stuck.** Mom suggests following their own tracks, but the ground is
   covered in old tracks and every hill is the same grey. Mom goes silent.
   *(The first science fact turns into the obstacle.)*
5. **The payoff.** Chie looks up at the Earth and remembers it was over the
   base when they left: drive toward the Earth. Mom doubts — surely the Earth
   crosses the sky like the Sun? Chie: not in the Moon's sky; she watches it
   every day. Mom turns the rover.
6. **Home.** From the hilltop they see the base lights; the radio comes back.
   Mom credits Chie.
7. **Coda.** That night Chie looks at the Earth from her window again — same
   hill, shining brightly — and it will stay there all through the long night.

## Japanese (prose)

ちえは十二歳で、月の基地にお母さんと住んでいる。ちえの部屋の窓からは、灰色の丘が見える。丘の上の黒い空には、いつも青い地球が浮かんでいる。地球は、空の同じ場所から動かない。ちえは一年間、毎日窓から地球を見ているので、それをよく知っている。窓の下の地面には、ローバーのタイヤの跡がたくさんある。月には風も雨もないので、跡はずっと消えない。

その日は、長い夜が始まる日だった。月の夜は、二週間も続く。夜になる前に、お母さんとちえはローバーで、基地の後ろの丘の向こうへソーラーパネルを調べに行った。「丘の向こうでは、基地と無線で話せないのよ」とお母さんは言った。出発する時、ちえは後ろを見た。基地の上の空に、青い地球が見えた。

ローバーは丘を越えて、まっすぐ走った。お母さんはパネルを一枚ずつ調べて、壊れたものを一つ直した。帰る途中で、ローバーの画面が急に消えた。お母さんはいろいろなボタンを押したけれど、画面は黒いままだった。地図がないので、基地がどちらにあるか分からなかった。「バッテリーも、あと一時間ぐらいしかない」とお母さんは言った。もうすぐ夜なので、太陽の光で充電することもできなかった。

「来た時のタイヤの跡をたどって帰ろう」とお母さんは言った。でも、外の地面を見て、二人は困った。地面は古いタイヤの跡でいっぱいで、どれが自分たちの跡か分からなかった。まわりの丘も、どれも同じ灰色だった。お母さんは、黙ってハンドルを握った。

ちえは、窓から空を見上げた。黒い空に、青い地球が浮かんでいた。ちえは、基地を出た時のことを思い出した。「お母さん、地球の方へ走って！」とちえは言った。「出る時、地球は基地の上にあった。だから、基地は地球の方にあるよ」。「でも、地球も太陽と同じで、空を動くでしょう？」とお母さんは聞いた。「ううん、月の空では動かない。毎日見ているから、知っているの」。お母さんはうなずいて、ローバーを地球の方へ向けた。

ローバーはゆっくり丘を登った。丘の上に着いた時、下に基地の明かりが見えた。無線から、「みんな心配したよ！」という声が聞こえた。お母さんは笑って、「ちえが地球を見て、道を見つけてくれたの」と言った。

その夜、ちえは部屋の窓から、また地球を見た。地球は、いつもと同じ丘の上で、明るく光っていた。長い夜の間も、地球はずっとそこにある。

## English

Chie is twelve, and she lives with her mother in a base on the Moon. From the window of her room, you can see a grey hill. In the black sky above the hill, the blue Earth is always hanging. The Earth never moves from its place in the sky. Chie has looked at it from her window every day for a year, so she knows this well. On the ground below the window there are lots of rover tyre tracks. The Moon has no wind and no rain, so the tracks never go away.

That day was the day the long night would begin. A night on the Moon lasts a whole two weeks. Before night fell, Mom and Chie took the rover out beyond the hill behind the base, to check the solar panels. “Past the hill, we can’t talk to the base by radio,” Mom said. As they set off, Chie looked back. The blue Earth was there in the sky above the base.

The rover went over the hill and drove straight on. Mom checked the panels one at a time and fixed one that was broken. On the way back, the rover’s screen suddenly went blank. Mom pressed all sorts of buttons, but the screen stayed black. Without the map, they couldn’t tell which way the base was. “And the battery only has about an hour left,” Mom said. Night was nearly here, so they couldn’t charge it with sunlight either.

“Let’s follow the tracks we made coming here,” Mom said. But when they looked at the ground outside, they didn’t know what to do. The ground was covered in old tyre tracks, and they couldn’t tell which ones were theirs. The hills all around were all the same grey, too. Mom gripped the steering wheel without a word.

Chie looked up at the sky through the window. The blue Earth was hanging in the black sky. Chie remembered the moment they left the base. “Mom, drive toward the Earth!” said Chie. “When we left, the Earth was above the base. So the base is over toward the Earth.” “But the Earth moves across the sky like the Sun does, doesn’t it?” Mom asked. “No, it doesn’t move in the Moon’s sky. I look at it every day, so I know.” Mom nodded and turned the rover toward the Earth.

The rover slowly climbed a hill. When they reached the top, they could see the lights of the base below. A voice came over the radio: “We were all so worried!” Mom laughed. “Chie looked at the Earth and found us the way home.”

That night, Chie looked at the Earth from her bedroom window again. It was shining brightly above the same hill as always. All through the long night, the Earth will stay right there.

## Read-through notes

- Every rule is planted before it pays off: the fixed Earth (¶1, ¶2 look-back),
  the tracks (¶1), the long night (¶2), the radio shadow of the hill (¶2).
- Chie solves it from her own year of watching, and Mom's objection (the Sun
  moves, so surely the Earth does) is the natural Earth-bound assumption.
- The tracks that "never disappear" look like a rescue and turn out to be the
  obstacle — the payoff is not luck.
- Grammar kept to G3: no conditionals (たら/ば/と/なら), no ながら, no
  そう/よう/らしい, no てしまう/てみる, no comparatives.
